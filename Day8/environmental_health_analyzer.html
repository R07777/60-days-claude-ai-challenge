Executive Summary — Bhubaneswar Environmental Profile
Air Quality: Bhubaneswar's annual average AQI stands at 104 (Moderate) per 2025 data (aqi.in historical analysis). In 2025, 0% of days met WHO safe air quality limits. Peak winter AQI can spike to 167–171 in January due to temperature inversion and construction dust. The primary pollutant is road dust from construction material transport, not vehicle emissions. PM10 commonly ranges 120–213 µg/m³ against a safe limit of 100 µg/m³. The AQLI estimates residents lose 2 years 7 months of life expectancy to air pollution.

Water Quality: Piped water supply across Bhubaneswar's 67 wards shows overall good to excellent quality per the 2025 PMC/KIIT study, but with localised deviations — 22.7% hardness excess, 10.5% pH deviation, and 19.2% DO deviation above WHO guidelines. 34 of 67 wards partially rely on groundwater which has quality concerns. Infrastructure aging causes contamination in some zones.

Trends: Air quality improves significantly during monsoon (Jun–Sep), with AQI dropping to the 50–80 range. Winter months (Nov–Feb) are worst. Water quality varies by zone — North Bhubaneswar has moderate concerns; southwest zones have relatively better water.

⚠️ Biggest Anomaly
Lucknow's AQI (152) spikes 2.5× above Pune (60) despite both being Tier-2 cities with comparable populations. Northern geography, temperature inversions, crop stubble burning from nearby Punjab/UP farms, and dense industrial activity explain this extreme divergence — not city size alone.
✨ Most Surprising Observation
Bhubaneswar's primary pollution source is NOT vehicle emissions — it's road dust from open-truck transport of construction materials. This means traffic-reduction policies have limited effect. Street-sweeping, covering construction trucks, and dust suppressants are far more impactful interventions per IQAir research.
